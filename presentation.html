The Classical Picture & the Puzzle

  The textbook view

On a degenerate optimum — a manifold of states with equal fitness — selection is silent. Motion along the manifold is treated as neutral genetic drift: unbiased, diffusive, going nowhere on average.

  The claim of this work

It is not neutral. The curvature of fitness transverse to the manifold creates a systematic, directional entropic drift — populations slide toward flatter regions even at equal fitness.

Equal fitness does not mean equal evolutionary stability.

A Minimal Degenerate Landscape

Two genes, one ridge of perfect fitness — and a curvature that varies along it.

f(x, y) = Fmax − ½ x² y²
  • Optimal manifold = both axes (x = 0 or y = 0): a cross of equal, maximal fitness.
  • Transverse curvature along the y-axis is |Hxx| = y² — sharp far out, flat near the origin.
  • Drift should push the population toward where the valley is widest.

  Confining variant

A second landscape f = Fmax − ½ x²(1+y²)² reverses the curvature so drift is confining. The theory predicts an entropic potential

Feff(y) = σ² ln(1 + y²)

whose stationary law along the manifold is a standard Cauchy — a clean, falsifiable prediction.

Four Dynamics, One Landscape

The repository implements and compares four ways a population can climb.

  Evolutionary Dynamics (ED)

Population-based evolution with selection + mutation. The reference biological process.

  Gradient Langevin (GLD)

Gradient ascent with additive isotropic noise — the canonical diffusion baseline.

  Stochastic Gradient (SGD)

Gradient ascent with shifted, state-dependent noise — a different drift signature.

  Natural Evolution Strategy

Natural-gradient ascent on expected fitness; compared under linear, multiplicative, and Boltzmann selection.

Where the Drift Comes From

Timescale separation: a fast transverse mode that the slow manifold mode rides on.

1. Fast mode equilibrates to a quasi-stationary variance set by local curvature:

Vx(y) = σ² / (1 + y²)²

2. The slow mode feels an entropic force — gradient flow down the effective potential:

d⟨y⟩/dτ = −2σ² y / (1 + y²) = − ∂y Feff(y)

The bias scales with the noise σ² and the gradient of transverse curvature — it vanishes only when the landscape is flat in every direction.

Closed-Form Expectations Under a Gaussian Population

For the x²y² landscape, every moment the theory needs is analytic.

Quantity Expectation under N(μ, Σ)
Fitness ⟨f⟩ Fmax − ½[(μx²+σx²)(μy²+σy²) + 2σxy² + 4μxμyσxy]
Gradient ⟨∂xf⟩ −μx(μy²+σy²) − 2μyσxy
Hessian ⟨∂xxf⟩ −(μy² + σy²)
Free energy (Boltzmann, temp. T) E = ln ⟨exp(f / T)⟩

Note the variance terms (σ²) sitting inside the mean gradient — that is the curvature–drift coupling, made explicit.

Takeaways

  • Degeneracy is not neutrality. Equal-fitness manifolds carry a systematic, curvature-induced drift.
  • The drift is entropic and predictable. Timescale separation gives a closed-form effective potential and stationary law (e.g. Cauchy).
  • The drift depends on the process. ED, GLD, and SGD settle in different places — the noise model matters.
  • Everything is reproducible. JAX implementations; every figure regenerable from one script on CPU.
